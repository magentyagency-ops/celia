So, the researchers wondered if these changes could also be found in the proteins present in tears.

In other words: could a simple tear give us information about type 1 diabetes in a child?

This is the question studied in a scientific article published in 2024 by a team of Greek researchers, Angelopoulou and colleagues, from the University of Athens, in the International Journal of Molecular Sciences.

02

Methods

How the study worked

Now, let’s see the methods.

112

children and teenagers

aged 6 to 17

Matched groups: 25 boys + 31 girls each

To answer this question, the researchers studied 112 children and teenagers between 6 and 17 years old.

[click] There were two groups: 56 children with type 1 diabetes and 56 healthy children.

Collecting tears

Schirmer strips under the lower eyelid

5 minutes
eyes closed
frozen at −20 °C

The researchers collected their tears using small Schirmer strips.

These are small strips of paper placed under the lower eyelid to collect tears.

[click] (Optional) The strips stayed 5 minutes, then were frozen until the analysis.

Mass spectrometry

Identifies and measures thousands of proteins.

1 peak = 1 molecule

Where → which one

How high → how much

In this study: liquid chromatography coupled to an Orbitrap mass spectrometer (LC-MS/MS).

They then analyzed the proteins present in the tears.

To do this, they used a technique called mass spectrometry.

In simple words, this technique allows scientists to identify and measure many different proteins in a sample.

Then, they compared…

HbA1c level

Average blood sugar (3 months)

Ketoacidosis

Blood too acidic at diagnosis

The researchers then compared the proteins found in the tears of children with diabetes with those found in healthy children.

[click] They also looked at some characteristics of the children with diabetes, such as their HbA1c level, which gives information about blood sugar control,

[click] and whether they had experienced diabetic ketoacidosis when they were diagnosed.

03

Results

What they found

Now, the results.

3,302

proteins found in tears

1 dot = 1 protein

239

are different in diabetes

≈ 7 %

The researchers found 3,302 different proteins in the children’s tears.

[click] When they compared the two groups, they found that 239 proteins were present in different amounts in children with type 1 diabetes compared with healthy children.

TFF1

Trefoil factor 1

Much higher in diabetes

Linked to protection and inflammation

One of the proteins that showed one of the biggest differences was called TFF1.

It was found in a much higher amount in the tears of children with diabetes.

44proteins

differ with ketoacidosis

e.g. PRDX2, PRDX6 ↓

The researchers also compared the children with diabetes according to different medical characteristics.

They found 44 proteins that were different between children who had experienced diabetic ketoacidosis when they were diagnosed and those who had not.

[click] (Optional) For example, two protective proteins, PRDX2 and PRDX6, were lower after ketoacidosis.

120proteins

differ with HbA1c

e.g. C4A ↑ with poor control

Finally, they compared the children according to their HbA1c level, which is a blood marker used to measure blood sugar control.

They also found differences in some tear proteins between these groups: 120 proteins.

[click] (Optional) For example, complement C4A, an immune protein, was higher when control was poor.

04

Discussion

What it means

Let’s discuss these results.

Diabetes changes tear proteins

Mostly linked to

the immune responseinflammationtissue balance

Better understand the disease

Tears reflect HbA1c and ketoacidosis

The results show that type 1 diabetes is associated with changes in tear proteins.

[click] Better understand the disease

[click] The researchers also found differences in proteins depending on the children’s HbA1c levels and whether they had experienced diabetic ketoacidosis. This suggests that tears could provide information about the disease.

Limitations

First

Only 112 children

Need more participants

Second

They already had diabetes

Early detection not proven

Finally

No tear test yet

More research needed

However, the study has some limitations.

First, only 112 children participated, so more participants would be needed to confirm the results.

[click] Second, the researchers studied children who already had diabetes, so they did not prove that tears could detect the disease early.

[click] Finally, more research is needed to develop a reliable test based on tears.

Conclusion

To conclude.

Promising…

In conclusion, this study is promising,

[click] but tear analysis cannot replace blood tests at the moment.
